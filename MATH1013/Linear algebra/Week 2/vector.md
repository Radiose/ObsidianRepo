vectors
In the last lecture, weve been looking at linear equations horizontally. IE for every equation, it has one row. 

A vector equation is when you view a [[matrix]] vertically. As in, $\begin{matrix}[1,2,3,4] \\ [1,2,2,2]\end{matrix}=\vec{v}\begin{pmatrix}1  \\ 1\end{pmatrix}+\vec{v}_{2}\begin{pmatrix}2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}+\vec{v_{3}}\begin{pmatrix}3  \\ 2\end{pmatrix} + \vec{v_{4}} \begin{pmatrix}4  \\  2\end{pmatrix}$

a singular vector can be directly translated into a set of coordinates in an n-dimensional plane.
It is very important that it is understood that vector coordinates are always relative. IE they are not fixed in reality. A point could be P could be given any set of coordinates based upon an arbitrary decision of where an origin is.
