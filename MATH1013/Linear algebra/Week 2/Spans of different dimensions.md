Spans of different dimensions
Span of dimension 1 
span$v_{1}=\{\mathbb{R}\ if\ v\not=0 \\  \}$ if v = 0 then $\vec{0}$

[[span]] of dimension 2
$\vec{v}_{1}\not=0,\vec{v}_{2}\not=0, \vec{v}_{1}$ not parallel to $\vec{v}_{2}$ for any $x \in \mathbb{R}$ (or that $\vec{v}_{1} \not= \vec{\lambda}v_{2}$). 
If these are true, then span($v_{1},v_{2}$) = $\mathbb{R}$
If $v_{1}$,$v_{2}$ are parallel, and at least one is nonzero, then span(v1,v2)= a line in $\mathbb{R}^2$, or a one dimensional [[subset]] of $\mathbb{R}^2$
if $\vec{v}_{1}=\vec{v}_{2}=0$, span{$v_{1},v_{2}$} ={$\vec{0}$}

[[span]] of dimension 3
if $v_{1}\not=v_{2}\not=v_{3}$ and also $\vec{v}_{1}\not=\lambda\vec{v}_{2}$ and $\vec{v}_{3}$does not lie on the 2 dimensional plane of $\vec{v}_{1}$ and $\vec{v}_{2}$, span{$\vec{v}_{1}\vec{v_{2}}\vec{v}_{3}$}=$\mathbb{R}^3$
Note, you can check if v_3 is on the three dimensional plane of v1 and v2 by plugging them all in a [[matrix]] and seeing if it results in a [[Consistent linear system]]. If it doesnt, then the span is $\mathbb{R}^3$, if it does, then span{v1,v2,v3}=a plane in $\mathbb{R}^3$
If $v_{1}=\lambda \vec{v}_{2}=\lambda \vec{v}_{3}$, then span = a line in $\mathbb{R}^3$

if $v_{1}=v_{2}=v_{3}=0$ span{v1,v2,v3}={$\vec{0}$}
