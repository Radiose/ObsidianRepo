Linear subspaces

The span of a set of [[vector]]s is a linear subspace
### Definition:
A subspace of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is a subset H of $\mathbb{R}^n$ that has the following properties 

the subset H passes through the point of origin - is also defined as $\vec{0} \in H$
- ensures that H is non empty 
for any u,v $\in H$, the sum of $u + v$ $\in H$
- H is closed under addition
for any $\vec{u} \in H$, and $c \in \mathbb{R}$, cu $\in H$
 - H is closed under multiplication

The idea behind the subspace concept is a space within a space 

Lines, planes etc in $\mathbb{R}^n$
 are subspaces if and only if they pass through the point of the origin

### examples 

is $\{[x] \in \mathbb{R} \ | \ x \ge 0 \}$ a subset of $\mathbb{R}?$
no, as any negative scalar will create a negative vector (doesnt follow the rules in the set)
Conclusion, the only linear subspaces of $\mathbb{R}$ are {$\vec{0}$} and $\mathbb{R}$ itself.

The only 1 dimensional subspace of $\mathbb{R}^2$ are lines passing through the point of origin

This basic concept applies for all subspaces. In $\mathbb{R}^3$, a plane that passes through the origin and extends infinitely out in all directions is a subspace.
