## Applications of [[The fundamental theorem of calculus]]

examples:
find the derivate of g, where $g(x) = \int_{0}^x \sqrt{ 1+t^2 }dt$
via *[[The fundamental theorem of calculus|FTC]] I*, $g'x = \sqrt{1+x^2}$

The lower bound is irrelevant for this, (unless its x in which case you flip the integral and reverse the sign as mention in [[Integration rules]]), as its a constant, and as a result can be subtracted ($\int_{b}^c = \int_{a}^c - \int_{a}^b$)

Find the derivative of g, where $g(x) = \int_{0}^{17} \sqrt{ 1+t^2 }dt$
note that this integrand is not a function of x, so it is considered a constant. Thus, the derivate of g(x) is 0.

$g(x) = \int_{0}^{\cos(x)} \sqrt{  1+t^2}$
The key here is to recognise these as the [[composition of Functions]], and from here, utilise the [[Chain rule]].
