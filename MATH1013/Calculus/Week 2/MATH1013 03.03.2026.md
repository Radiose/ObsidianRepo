A horizontal asymptote can occur as you get the limit of a function as it approaches infinity. 

as x approaches -infinity or +infinity, a horizontal asymptote is formed. 
For example, $\lim_{ x \to \infty }e^x=0$, therefore $e^x$ has a horizontal asymptote at 0.

discontinuities
A removable discontinuity is when a function is defined with a discontinuity can be simply redefined. 

a jump discontinuity is when the left limit, and the right limit exists. However, they are not equal. Therefore, there is no limit at point a. 

An infinite discontinuity at x = a if x = a is a vertical asymptote of y = f(x), that is, at least one of the one sided limits is infinity. 

An essential discontinuity exists if at least one of its one sided limits at x=a does not exist as is not even infinite.
