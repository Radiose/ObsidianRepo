Centre mass of a thin rod 

The Law of the Lever states that when a force is applied to a lever arm, the
magnitude of the turning force created is equal to the force times the distance
to the fulcrum

We will use the Law of the Lever to locate the centre of mass of a thin rod
whose density varies.


Suppose that a thin rod lies along the x-axis between x = a and x = b, with x
measured in cm. Suppose further that the density of the rod is modelled by the
function $ρ(x)$ whose unit is g · cm−1

Let c denote the centre of mass (the balancing point) of the the rod. We aim to determine this. 



We create a partition and a set of sample points 
To find the force, we have to sum the mass off all partitions up to the point we have and multiply it by g $f = mA$. (Find the mass of the entire rod up to the point we have).

We decide that any mass left of c will be negative, and any to the right will be positively signed.

Because we have a function for density, we use this to get the mass, which is done by $\rho(x_{i^*})\times \Delta x$. This is proportional because we note that this is a *thin* rod, and as such we simplify our model to only take in two dimensions. This also works because we assume that density is constant through each interval. 
Thus we end up with the integrand $\int_{a}^b (x-c)g\rho(x)dx$. We equate this to zero to solve for C, which will be the centre point(signed force at midpoint is zero)
