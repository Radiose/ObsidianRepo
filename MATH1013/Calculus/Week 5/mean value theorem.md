![[Rolles theorem]]Mean value theorem 
Let f be a [[function]] that satisfies the following hypothesis 
1: f is [[continuous on an interval]] \[a,b]
2: F is differentiable on the open interval (a,b)
Then there exists at least one number c such that 
$f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$

its basically saying that there exists a mean value to these two points (*a,f(a)* and *(b,f(b))*) being the derivative of another point. OR, more officially *that there exists a point c such that its instantaneous rate of change is equal to the average rate of change from \[a,b]* if and only if f **satisfies the** **hypothesis.** 



The mean value theorem demonstrates that if$f'(x) = 0$ for all $x$ in interval $(a,b)$, then $f$ is constant on $(a,b)$.
