The average rate of change can be defined as $\frac{f(x)-f(x_{2})}{x-x_{2}}$ or units of f(x) / units of x

formal definition of a derivative:
$\lim_{ h \to 0 }\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$
$\lim_{ x \to a }\frac{f(a)-f(x)}{a-x}$


A function is considered differentiable at x if f'(x) exists. If f'(x) exists for all $x \in (a,b)$, we say that f is differentiable in (a,b). 

IE: if f is differentiable at a, then it is continuous at a.
BUT, just because a function is continuous, does not mean it is differentiable. IE |x| at 0.
The difference quotient is the formal definition of a derivative without the limit.



The derivate of a constant is 0 = $\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}=\frac{x-x}{h}$  for a constant. ()

The derivate of cosx = -sin x
the derivate of sinx = cosx
