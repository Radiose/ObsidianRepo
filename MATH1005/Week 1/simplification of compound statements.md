simplification of compound statements
For statement variables p and q:

$\neg(\neg p) = p$
$\neg(p \land q) \equiv \neg p \lor \neg q$
$\neg(p \implies q) \equiv p \land \neg q$ - this [[logical equivalence]] is very important. This is because the only false is when q is true and p is false for implies. You cannot reverse it, as that would be the inverse.
$p \leftrightarrow q \equiv (p \land \neg q)\lor(\neg p \land q)$

each of these [[logical equivalence]]s can be proven using [[TRUTH TABLES]].

The right hand side of each logical equivalence shown has the property that $\neg$ appears only in front of statement variables.
