The conditional statement:

p implies q:

The tricky part of this is T F  = F
and F T = T

If today is wednesday, then Im a gold medalist. Since today is not wednesday, the statement is true. 

for $p \implies q$, p is known as the hypothesis, and q is known as the conditional.
negating an implication:
$\neg(p \implies q)\equiv$
$p \land \neg q$
This is because $a \implies b \equiv (\neg a) \lor b$



When the hypothesis is FALSE, and the conditional is true, the conditional is **vacuously true**

More on implies:

You show up for work and you will get the job:

You show up and get the job - true
you show up dont get the job - false 
You dont show up - vacuously true

you dont show up and get the job cannot be proven as false, as he never stated that not showing up **wont** give you the job.

$p \implies q$ can also be read aloud in each of the following ways:

*p* implies *q*
if *p* then *q*
*p* only if *q*
*q if p*



relationships between implications for implies:

$p \implies q$ converse is $q \implies p$
$p \implies q$ inverse $\neg p \implies \neg q$

$p \implies q$ contrapositive $\neg q \implies \neg p$

$\neg(p \implies q) \equiv p \implies \neg q$


Important facts
The inverse is logically equivalent to the converse
The statement is not logically equivalent to its converse
the statement is logically equivalent to its contrapositive
