Proving the biconditional 
to prove $p \leftrightarrow q$, you may first prove $p \implies q$ and $q \implies p$
It is possible to accomplish both directions of proof simultaneously by arguing with biconditionals throughout your proof, but you must be careful when doing so.

example: for all integers x, x is even if and only if $x^2+2$ is even

Recognise the [[logical connectives]] and the [[quantification]] present
$\forall x(x is even \leftrightarrow x^2+x is even)$
proof: let x be in an integers
For the $\to$ direction, we argue directly

Hence $x^2+x$ is even
for the $\leftarrow$ direction, we argue via the contrapositive.

Hence $x^2+x$ is not even
