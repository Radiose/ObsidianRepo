SUFFICIENT AND NECESSARY CONDITIONS


P is a sufficient condition for Q means $p \implies q$
P is a necessary condition for Q means $\neg p \implies \neg q$
because $(\neg p \implies \neg q )\equiv (q \implies p)$, you can say:
p is a necessary condition for q means  $q \implies p$

A condition is both sufficient and necessary when it uses the biconditional statement 

p is a necessary AND sufficient condition for p means $p \leftrightarrow q$

in view of the above terminology, statements of the form $p \implies q$ and $p \leftrightarrow q$ are often referred to as [[The conditional statement]]s.



Being older than 16 is a necessary condition for being allowed to
drive. It is not sufficient.

Having a drivers license is a necessary and sufficient condition for
being allowed to drive.

Being a taxi driver is a sufficient condition for being allowed to drive.
It is not necessary.

It is important to note that a necessary condition is not sufficient and a sufficient condition is not necessary.
