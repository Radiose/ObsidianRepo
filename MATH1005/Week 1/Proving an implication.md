Proving an implication
To prove $p \implies q$
Suppose that p is true
Deduce that by valid and explicit reasoning, that q must be true. 
This is called arguing directly

You may also ague via the contrapositive
suppose that $\neg q$ is true 
Deduce that by valid and explicit reasoning, that $\neg p$ must be true

for example 
for all integers x, if x is even, then $x^2 + 2$ is even
The first step here is to recognise the structure of this [[Statement]] uses if... then, which means it uses [[The conditional statement]], with [[quantification]].
  $\forall x(x is even \implies x^2+2 is even)$

Follow the [[logical structure of a proof]]
Proof: let x be an integer. We shall argue directly, suppose that x is even.
...
hence, $x^2+2$ is even

or 

Proof: let x be an integer. We shall argue via the contrapositive. Suppose that x is not even. By the even/odd [[theorem]], x is odd.

Hence $x^2+2$ is odd. By the even/odd theorem, $x^2+2$ is not even
