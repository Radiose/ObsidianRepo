# EXAMPLE
For tossing three fair coins, what is the [[Sample space]]?
- $(T +H)(T+H)(T+H)$
	=$\{ TTT \},\{ TTH \},\{ THH \},\{ HTH \},\{ HTT \},\{ HHT \},\{ THT \},\{ HHH \}$
What [[subset]]s of the sample space contain the following events: 1 head, 2 heads, 3 heads 
$E_{1}=\{TTH,THT,HTT  \}\ \ E_{2}=\{ THH \}, \{HHT  \},\{ HTH \}\ \ E_{3}={HHH}$
What is the size of the [[Sample space]]?
- 8
What do we divide frequencies by on the [[Frequency Histogram]] to get a [[Density function]]? 
- 8
What are the probabilities of the events? 
$\frac{3}{8}, \frac{3}{8}, \frac{1}{8}$


The example shown above is an example of a **binomial** density/distribution 
As the sample space increases, the binomial probability [[Density function]] gets closer and closer to the famous **Bell curve**, which is also known as the **Normal [[Density function]]
![[Pasted image 20260503161531.png]]

Its convergence is a consequence of the **Central limit theorem**.
