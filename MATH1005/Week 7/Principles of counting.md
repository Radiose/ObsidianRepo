Principles of counting
How: we have a collection of counting principles. When we need to count some objects, we analyse those objects until we carefully match the situation to one of the situations in which a counting principle applies. 

Why its hard: matching a scenario to one of the scenarios described in counting principle takes care, as the scenarios described in the counting principles sound similar unless you read carefully. 

Best strategy: understand scenarios described in counting principles. Carefully notice how the scenarios are different. Then, when you have to count something and you think the counting principle applies, state which principle, explain it carefully how you know it can apply.

Principles:
[[Bijective|bijection]]s preserve [[cardinality]]
If A and B are finite sets and there exists a [[Bijective|bijection]] from A to B, then A and B have the same [[cardinality]] ($|A| = |B|$)
