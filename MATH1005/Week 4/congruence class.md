congruence class
for any $d \in \mathbb{N}$ and any $a \in \mathbb{Z}$, the congruence class$[a]_{d}$ of modulo d is defined as $\{ m \in \mathbb{Z} | \ m \equiv a (mod \ d)\}$

for any $a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2} \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $d \in \mathbb{N}$
$a_{1} \equiv a_{2} \ (mod\  d) \implies a_{1}\pm b_{1}\equiv a_{2}\pm b_{2}$
$b_{1} \equiv b_{2} (mod \ d)$ $\iff a_{1}\times b_{1} \equiv a_{2}\times b_{2} \ (mod \ d)$

### Simplifying [[congruence modulo]]

When computing modulo d, you may, at any time, replace a number by something to which it is equivalent. In this way, you can simplify calculations.

For example

$379-803 (mod \ 7)$
we know that 
$379 \equiv 1$ (mod 7)
and 
$803 \equiv 5$ (mod 7)
so therefore, it is the same as
$1-5$ mod 7 $\equiv$ 1 - 5 $\equiv$ -4
Same thing as $803^5$
$803 \equiv 5^5 \equiv 80 \equiv 3$ (mod 7)
