We claim that between any two numbers there is a rational number(in fact infinitely many) and an irrational number (in fact infinitely many)

Demonstration:
suppose $0 < a < b$
Choose an integer n such that  $\frac{1}{n}<b-a$, then at least one member of the [[Sequence]]
$$ \frac{1}{n}, \frac{2}{n}, \frac{3}{n}, \frac{4}{n}\dots$$ will lie between a and b.

To see this, take the first integer m such that $a < \frac{m}{n}$, it will follow that $\frac{m}{n}< b$

Geometrically, ![[Pasted image 20260618181043.png]]
Since 1/n is less than b-a, there exists some integer multiple of n that fits in between that space. 

If a < 0, a similar argument follows using $-\frac{1}{n},-\frac{2}{n}\dots$

Finally, choosing n so $\frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{n}<b-a$ and applying a similar argument to the sequence 
$$ \frac{\sqrt{ 2}}{n}, \frac{2\sqrt{ 2 }}{n}\dots$$ will yield the result for irrational numbers.
