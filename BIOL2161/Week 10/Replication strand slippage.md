Replication strand slippage

This is a way of losing a gaining nucleotides in DNA 
This is when a strand during [[DNA replication|replication]] bulges out and a nucleotide is left unpaired to a complementary one. 

![[Pasted image 20260513125843.png]]

The gain of it is when the replicated strand has the bulge, while the loss of nucleotides occur when the template strand gets a bulge. 

Alot of [[Base excision repair]] mechanisms will take care of this, but occasionally they can slip through. 
## Generation of Tandem Repeats
Replication slippage causes the generation of [[Tandemly repeated DNA]], particularly the expansion of trinucleotide repeats.  Because there are more hydrogen bonds between CG than AT, a strand with GTCGTC... can get a hairpin structure created. The bulging out of the trinucleotide repeats will lead to more when DNA polymerase fills it out in [[DNA replication|replication]].

Many diseases can be caused by the expansion of trinucleotide repeats.
