Alu element

This is an element in DNA derived from a transposon. It has around one million copies in the human genome, and arose 65 million years ago in the primate lineage. About 1 in 20 live births has a new alu element insertion.  
Alu elements dont encode for any proteins.  They just retain recognition sequences.  The retainment of these leads to production of the reverse transcriptase by "borrowing" from L1 elements

## Insertion into other genes 
The crossing over of [[DNA]] in [[holliday junctions]] causes insertion of ALU elements into genes. The sister chromatids exchange. Regions of homologous DNA lead to crossing over, [[Transposable element]]s create new regions of homologous [[DNA]]. 

## Effect on gene expression 
Insertion of an [[Alu element]] into an [[Introns and exons|intron]] changes the way a gene is spliced. 
They contain CPG motifs - typically targeted by [[DNA methylation]], leading to gene silencing. 
Some alus contain promoters or binding sites for [[Transcription factor]]s - boosting [[Transcription]]
