Transposable elements
These are [[DNA]] elements that can move around the [[Genome]]. 
Scientists saw the heritability of genes were not stable. Genes that were close together typically were inherited together. Scientists thought that elements were getting moved around in the gene. 

 DNA transposons are moderately repeated, mobile DNA sequences that are spread throughout the [[Genome]]s of [[prokaryotic cells]], plants and animals alike.   You can observe between individuals that different numbers were present and that they occupied different spaces in the genome 

Transposons range in size from hundreds to a few thousand BP 
The sequences are cut and inserted into a new site in the genome by the process of **transposition**

44% of the human genome is comprised of [[Transposable element]], corn has 85%, fruit flies have 3%. However, much of the transposable elements we and fruit flies have are inactive. 

There are two classes of [[Transposable element]]
# Class 1(retrotransposons)
Retrotransposons operate via a *copy and paste* mechanism
This is done simply by creating an RNA transcript which is then reverse transcribed into [[DNA]] - done via [[RNA]] intermediate 
Some transposon derived sequences include:
retrotransposons - for example L1 or ALU - come back and find 
We have long interspersed nuclear elements, and short (LINES and SINES)

For this to take place, we need a *reverse transcriptase* to produce a [[DNA]] [[RNA]] hybrid (heteroduplex). This will then be converted to double stranded DNA by degradation of RNA and synthesis of a new DNA strand (via DNA pol).

So a retrotransposon will encode a reverse transcriptase. We get the RNA molecule, which encodes for production of reverse transcriptase, which then converts it to the hybrid.

![[Alu element]]


Retrotransposons occur also in [[Introns and exons|intron]]s rather than  just exons. The addition of introns lead to lots of changes in exons as well. 
## Class 2(DNA transposons)
DNA transposons operate via a *cut and paste* mechanism 
We recognise, cut and reinsert the transposon 

DNA transposon have terminal inverted repeats, and a 9 bp flanking direct repeat. We have flanking direct repeats which are important for recognition by transposing proteins 
The coding region minimally encodes a **transposase enzyme** but can also encode other proteins. Genes are carried along with the transposon.
