Alternative splicing
This is a unique case that allows for diversity in eukaryotic cell transcription. A [[Transcription unit]]'s transcript will not always be processed the same way during [[RNA splicing|mRNA processing]]. 

This occurs in a variety of ways.

### Alternative exon
One of the ways that this could occur is simply an exon getting spliced out in the middle of a transcript. ![[Pasted image 20260420111317.png]]

We can see exon 3 is processed out in mRNA 2, while included in mRNA 1.



Sometimes two exons both have poly a tails, but one is discluded for whatever reason.

![[Pasted image 20260420111459.png]]

On first glance, this looks like it could be a different form of alternative splicing, but because mRNA2 does not include mRNA 1, what occured is that mRNA 2 had exon 2 transcribed, but it was then processed out.  If there was a form of mRNA with both of them present, it would be instead alternative 3' end processing. 

### Alternative 3' end processing

![[Pasted image 20260420112238.png]]

This is a form of splicing that occurs when different poly-a sites result in different ends to the transcript. It differs to exon splicing, as there is never a gap in the transcript. All exons to the terminus are translated, while in exon splicing, there can be gaps in the middle. 

### Alternative promoter 

![[Pasted image 20260420112730.png]]


This is similar to the last one, at the promoter end instead of the terminus. *However*, there is a key difference here, which is that exons can be discluded in the middle of a chain. This is because you cannot access an exon attached to a promoter region unless you utilise that promoter region. For this reason, mRNA1 skips over [[Introns and exons|exon]] 1b. 

 [[Alternative splicing]] is Done as the RNA synthesis proceeds. Occurs during [[Elongation of Transcription in Eukaryotic cells]].

Is very important. Without it, ribosome will not be able to properly translate the protein. 


# Mechanism
Splicing is the process by which [[Introns and exons|intron]]s are removed from pre- [[mRNA]] and exons are joined to produce a continuous coding sequence. It is carried by a large RNA-protein complex called the spliceosome. Generally done 5'-3'.

Even though most splicing is done [[Transcription]], a significant amount of introns at the 3' end are spliced out post transcription, and sometimes it doesnt follow 5'-3' direction. 

Each [[Introns and exons|intron]] contains three critical elements for the splicing:

Donor site - 5' splice site- typically starts with GU - is a [[Consensus sequence]]

branch point - sequence containing a conserved adenosine, 14-80 nucleotides from acceptor site  - 

Acceptor site  - 3' splice site - ends in AG - is another [[Consensus sequence]]



Mechanism of splicing 
1: [[RNA]] is cleaved at the 5' splice site separating exon 1 and the intron. 
2: Lariat is formed. This is when that 5' site is now connected to the branch site in a loop.
3: cut at 3' end
4: now intron is released, 5' end detaches from A and degradation of intron occurs 
5: two exons are now joined 

This splicing occurs via transesterification reaction 
 - breaks one phosphodiester bond and forms another 
 - no ATP required
