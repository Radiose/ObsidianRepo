### Intron 
These are regions of DNA which do not code for anything. Present in [[Eukaryotic cells]]. Introns are highly variable in size, 3-5kbp. Most of the [[Genome structure]] of [[Eukaryotic cells|eukaryote]]s are comprised of introns. Not all genes in eukaryotes have introns, but most do. A question that has stumped biologists for a long time is why do [[Eukaryotic cells|eukaryote]] genes have introns in the first place? ### Enable [[Alternative splicing]]
Introns allow for [[Alternative splicing]] to occur, and allows for high levels of regulation. 

Introns also contribute to [[Gene regulation]] directly. They contain [[Enhancer sequence]]s, silencers, and binding sites. 

They facilitate [[Introns and exons|exon]] shuffling. Recombination between introns can create new gene architectures.

They allow for their presence to indicate faulty transcription.
