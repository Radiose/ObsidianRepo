Post translation regulation 
Protein function post [[Translation]] can be regulated after its made via chemical modifications or controlled degradation. 
The protein regulations can be done quickly and reversibly and so they can be used to fine tune protein activity, localisation and stability. 

This regulation is achieved through modifications to selected amino acids or proteolytic cleavage. 

There are many many types of modifications. There is no general recipe for what a modification will do.

 Post translational modifications all are added by specific enzymes that recognise target proteins and modify them in response to cellular signals. 


## Examples
Phosphorylation: inhibits or activates protein activity: kinases 

Ubiquitination: Targets proteins for degradation: Ligases 

Acetylation: Alters protein interactions: Acetyltransferases 

Methylation: modulates protein interactions: Methyltransferases 

Glycosylation: alters protein folding, stability, secretion: Glycosyltransferases 

Lipidation: Targets membrane proteins: Lipid transferases

Hydroxylation: Regulates stability and function: hydroxylases

Proteolytic cleavage: Activates or matures protein(irreversible): proteases
