### Function in bacteria 
recognise foreign [[DNA]], cuts it to prevent infection 

Crispr uses RNA to guide DNA cutting via Cas9 
Not a native [[Gene regulation]] mechanism in [[Eukaryotic cells|eukaryote]]s.



[[CRISPR]] is a fast, precise and versatile gene editing technology in [[Eukaryotic cells|eukaryote]]s which has been adapted from a natural bacterial immune system. It uses a guide gRNA to direct a [[Double stranded DNA break]] in the targeted DNA. The native cellular repair machinery repairs the break resulting in [[nonhomologous end joining]] or Homology-Directed Repair (HDR).

NHEJ introduces mutations (indels) that often
disrupt the coding sequence, resulting in loss of
functional protein. This is CRISPR mechanism
used to silence, switch-off a specific gene.

HDR can insert a user-designed fragment of donor
DNA into the specific location cut by Cas9. The
donor DNA must have 'homology arms' – flanking
sequences homologous to the both sides of the cut
location to allow the DNA repair mechanism
pasting it in.
