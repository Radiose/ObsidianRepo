Promoter proximal pausing

After the [[Initiation of transcription in Eukaryotic cells]], and before the [[Elongation of Transcription in Eukaryotic cells]], an extra step of processing must occur. 

Recall that after ~30 bp of mRNA synthesis, pol II leaves the [[promoter region]] and the [[Transcription factor]] and enters the assembly line mode, elongating mRNA using DNA as template.

Typically, pol II will pause. 

Promoter proximal pausing is the step in [[Transcription of Eukaryotic cells]] where [[RNA polymerase II]] will initiate, transcribe ~30 nucleotides, then pause before [[Elongation of Transcription in Eukaryotic cells]]. This state allows for increased [[Gene regulation]] - everything is ready for transcription to occur extremely quickly. Upon a signal via [[Transcription factor]]s, it will begin transcription. 

### Regulation
The major protein responsible is called *P-TEFb*, which phosphorylates [[RNA polymerase II|pol II]], allowing it to begin elongation. Its regulation is via [[Transcription factor]]s, bound to [[Enhancer and silencer sequence|enhancer sequence]]s or [[promoter region]]. This is typically used when a fast response is needed without rebuilding the [[Initiation of transcription in Eukaryotic cells|transcription machinery]]. 

### Causes
Genes can stay primed sometimes seconds, minutes or more. 
Heat shock response, stress response can lead to PPP being needed.
