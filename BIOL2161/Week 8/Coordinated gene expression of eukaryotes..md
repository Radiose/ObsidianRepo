Coordinated gene expression of eukaryotes. 
In [[Eukaryotic cells|eukaryote]]s, there are no [[Operon]]s, so coordinated gene expression is significantly more difficult. 
Multiple gene expression is required to react to stress. 
[[Operon]]s are very very rarely used in [[Eukaryotic cells]]. In many cases, genes in the same pathway are associated to separate [[Topologically associating domain|TAD]]s. 

The concept with coordinated gene expression in eukaryotes is that a single signal, or a single transcription factor can regulate more than one gene. 

Coordinated gene expression is typically done via shared [[Transcription factor]]s, as genes that must be expressed together will have similar TF binding sites. 
The other method is when a signal(hormone or stress) activates multiple TFs at once. These TFs will then regulate a set of [[Gene]]s simultaneously.

## Example
Glucocorticoid Receptor
A [[Transcription factor]] regulating hundreds to thousands of genes. 
In the absence of glucocorticoid hormone (GH), each of these [[Gene]]s have its level of expression already regulated by other TFs. These TFs alone produce low-level expression, thus they are regulated independently generally. 

When GH enters the cell, it binds with GR, and activates it. The GR now binds [[DNA]], and interacts with TF's already in place, increasing expression of all genes.
