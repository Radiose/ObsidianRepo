Gene regulation
Not all genes are appropriate to be expressed all the time.
Genes should be turned off unless required in order to match cell energetic demands and survival.

![[Operon]]

 Promoter strength 
 A factor that controls how much an [[Operon]] is transcribed.
A promoter is a regulatory DNA sequence
	IE sequence of the promoter controls how well RNA polymerase can bind - efficiency and efficacy(eg are -10 and -35 regions )
A strong promoter will lead to more [[mRNA]]
weak will lead to less

Operator 
genetic sequence that allows proteins that are responsible for transcription to attach to the [[DNA]] sequence 
Operators often act as binding sites for regulatory proteins that promote or inhibit [[Transcription]]

A generic example 
regulator gene is transcribed and translated into a protein 
[[RNA]] polymerase will be blocked when regulatory protein binds to operator 

Inducible [[Operon]]
inducible means [[Transcription]] is normally turned **off**, but can be turned **on** by an inducer 
Repressible means [[Transcription]] is normally **on**, but can be turned **off** by an inducer 

![[Transcription control]]

negative inducible vs negative repressor 
negative inducible operon - transcription is normally off, under negative control, so regulatory protein is repressor. In presence of inducer, it will get repressor off
Negative repressible: in presence of inducer, repressor binds to operator and turns gene off.
