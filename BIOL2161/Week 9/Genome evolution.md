Genome evolution
# Prokaryotes
In [[prokaryotic cells]], genomes typically evolve via **horizontal gene transfer**, the gain of genes from other organisms. These mechanisms are usually prokaryote [[plasmid]] transfer, Viruses exploding cells (cell lysis). They also can randomly take up [[DNA]] from the environment.

Gene loss is selected for in evolution. Smaller genomes allow faster cell duplication and thus smaller genes are needed.



# [[Eukaryotic cells|eukaryote]]s
Presence of introns will actually somewhat help an organism. The presence of introns give you the ability to produce multiple variations of protein, which aids in complexity. New genes can evolve via [[Exon shuffling]].

Using [[Multigene family]]s allow for much faster genome evolution. Much faster to repurpose old genes rather evolve new ones from scratch




### Effect of [[Transposable element|transposon]]s
There are indirect and direct effects of transposons on evolution. 
They can directly cause mutations because of where they insert and what they drag along with them - can effect any sort of regulatory outcome you can think of

Indirectly through [[homologous recombination]] through retrotransposons.
