# Epigenetics and evolution 
The Centromeres and telomeres are constitutive hetero[[Chromatin]]. 

This is important, in [[homologous recombination]], crossover events tend to colocalise with [[Gene]]s, [[promoter region]]. This only occurs in regions of open [[Chromatin]] and low levels of [[DNA methylation]]. 


How do we study differences in [[DNA methylation]]? We treat some genetic material with sodium bisulphite that will convert unmethylated cytosine to uracil. In the no treatment sample, we see unmethylated cytosines being retained. It allows us to see what cytosines are methylated. 

We can also use *nanopore sequencing*. There is a larger charge differential when methylated cytosine crosses the pore, vs when unmethylated cytosine crosses the pore. 


Feedback between [[DNA methylation]] and [[Chromatin remodelling]].
Methylated DNA recruits different proteins - this leads to [[Chromatin remodelling]] and vice versa. 


Zebularine treatment: 
Zebularine will inhibit methyltransferase. You can utilise this to identify wheat epi mutants. It appears that [[DNA methylation]] leads to a recruitment of HDAC's which would favour [[Chromatin]] packaging. The loss of this will lead to lots of [[homologous recombination]], leading to [[Gene]] deletion. 


Epigenetic and disease 
[[Epigenetic]] regulation has a role in [[Cancer]]
There are many genes that are *tumour suppressant [[Gene]]s*. By having methylation in tumour suppressor genes, [[Cancer]] will be caused. 
Some carcinogens will cause methylation of tumour suppressor genes. 
Other carcinogens can cause demethylation of oncogenes. 

Some drugs are able to remove methylation and treat the cancer. It will inhibit DNA methyltransferase, and lead to a reactivation of a tumour suppressor gene. 

Smoking can lead to [[Epigenetic]] changes in a body. Smoking while pregnant can lead to [[Epigenetic]] changes in the fetus.
