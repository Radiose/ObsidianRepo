Report writing
**Writing a good lab report**:
Remember your audience - adapt writing style to each scientific journal - plant vs cancer articles are very different. The aim is to comminate what you found out to a reader who is not familiar with your experiment.  

Keep it clear and concise - make it accessible to a wider audience by making it short, clear and concise. Do not use big words for no reason
Use appropriate scientific language - do not misrepresent scientific concepts by oversimplifying

biol2161:
organisms should be introduced using the full scientific name first, then abbreviated for the rest of the report
Organism names should always be italicised. 
Gene names should be italicised, but not protein names EG *LAC Z* genes. 
*E.coli* is two words 
Follow rubric to a TEE 
NO abstract or methods. 
No references: focus is on analysizing you data because the principles are well established. Focus on what you get from experiment, not what you can find elsewhere,
References are optional. All theory necessary is from lab book. If you want to use theory from elsewhere, harvard style. 

first year vs second year lab reports:
more about structure in first year
second year some bits not included. Same overall structure but different focus. **Focus is on understanding an analysing more complex experiments, and using data to support a conclusion**
More emphasis on results and discussion, rather than having comparisons to literature. This is more based on the experiments results. 


Report 1
**DO NOT INCLUDE x-gal screen in report 1. Focus on killing curves.**

TItle: refers to content of report, and not anything else
scientific title
introduction:
What did you do - talk about theory of methods without talking about actual methods - IE how x gal can be used
What did you do it to - DNA repair mechanisms in E coli, etc
Why did you do it? - relate it to something else - only a sentence or two

Aim:
relevant, clearly stated and spefific - one to 2 sentences - Organism (e.coli) wildtype/mutant, what strategy was used

Results:
Using appropriate text to introduce tables and graphs: wants to read like a story. Tell reader why each graph is used. Cells were irradiated for x amount of time see table 1. 
Presenting graphs and tables clearly and accurately with descriptive tables: Needs to be labelled, tables need to be titled, graphs need detailed figure caption, with first sentence the title. 
Results presented in text:
Describe but do not discuss data. Dont need to go through every single individual point, but highlight the key numbers. EG calculating 90% kill times
Controls used effectively. Can be used in discussion as well. Ensure that positive and negative controls are accurately described. Can have more than 1 control.

Discussion:
40 marks. largest part IE most words
State whether aims are being met, whether hypothesis is supported
Restate aim. These were achieved/not achieved
Supporting conclusions with reference to appropriate data. Reference own data. ""The values were this and this"
Provide logical argument leading to conclusions, drawing on theoretical background. Information is relevant and controls are discussed.
Combine uncertainty of conclusions and suggest an appropriate further experiment. Do not talk about human error. Talk about actual missing aspects of experiment. Suggest experiment to bride 

Presentation, style and self assessment. 
Report is 1000 words, 10% leeway
Space is used effectively - put words in appropriate section IE not all in introduction and none in discussion

Make sure report is written clearly, grammar and spelling is correct 
Terminology relating to genes and proteins used correctly. IE translation and transcription. 
Self assessment section: 3 questions. 
What part of the report did you find most challenging
What is something you think you did well in this report
Where would you like more directed feedback
These questions are included in word count
-5 marks for each working day late without an approved extension.
