Chromatin
Chromatin is the material that comprises [[Chromosome]]s. It is described as a dense mixture of [[DNA]] and proteins([[histone]]) that package long DNA molecules into compact, dense structures within the nucleus. 

The fibre that builds the [[DNA]] together. 

Euchromatin is the extended form of chromatin.  - loosely packed for [[DNA replication]]
Heterochromatin is the condensed form of chromatin - tightly packed - inactive form

The state of chromatin determines whether a gene is available for [[Transcription]]. It must be euchromatin, as a gene located in heterochromatin is effectively off. 

For a [[Gene]] to be transcribed, the promoter and key regulatory elements must be accessible, and the gene body must be in a chromatin state that allows for [[RNA polymerase II]] to pass. 

### Remodelling

Starting transcription requires a fully accessible [[promoter region]], while elongation can occur through chromatin that is actively being remodelled. [[RNA polymerase II]] can unwind nucleosomes itself. 

Chromatin can be remodelled via several mechanisms 
[[histone]] modifications:
- Histone acetylation - opens chromatin up and promotes [[Transcription]]
- Histone methylation - activates or represses transcription - is context dependent. 

DNA methylation: Modifications to DNA directly. It acts as a signal that recruits proteins which compact chromatin and silence gene expression. 

All chromatin remodelling is done via proteins with regulatory signals. These proteins are called **transcription factors**
