All genetically edited plants are also technically transgenes because they have antibiotic resistance in them. As a result, we select the progeny, and due to mendelian segregation, some will not have the transgene, which is non GM. (but still genome edited). We can now use it for further analysis.


There is still 4-6 years required - needs a year to create first gen plant - then needs to flower.

Transgene segregation takes the longest. Mendelian segregation if each gene has a single copy. 
Sometimes, you have genes with hundreds of copies, which requires lots and lots of screening. In canola for example can occur in first generation, other times it can take forever. 
The other reason: 

Somaclonal variation: Mutations in tissue culture often have genetic changes and mutations called somaclonal variation. 
We remove these by crossing these back into the parent plant. 
All genome editing happens via tissue culture  - easy to validate and easy to scale 
Cons - Not well developed tissue culture - transgene insertion(could alter lots of things that arent wanted could mess up genome architecture) and somaclonal variation which is the worst
