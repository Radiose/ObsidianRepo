Cancer

cancer arise from uncontrolled cell division - this is caused by mutations in somatic cells
Cells become cancerous only after accumulating more than one mutation 
Inherited cancer are often due to mutations in [[DNA]] repair pathways - resulting in a higher mutation rate

## Case study 


Cancer is essentially a failure of [[Gene regulation]]. In normal healthy tissue, cell division is tightly regulated and damaged cells are repaired or removed. Cells will receive signals from other cells, and tissue homeostasis is maintained. 

However, in cancer cells, they ignore anti cell division signals and continue dividing despite [[DNA]] damage. They avoid apoptosis, and accumulate additional [[mutation]]s.


## Monoclonal origin 
All tumours are monoclonal in origin. They all originate from a single abnormal cell that acquires mutations allowing uncontrolled proliferation.
#### general process 
- A normal cell acquires a mutation
- The mutated cell gains a selective advantage 
- Descendent cells inherit the mutation 
- Additional mutations accumulate over time 
- A malignant tumour eventually develops 


## Types of mutations 
Activation of proto oncogenes resulting in continuous growth signalling, causing proliferation without external signals 

Loss of tumour suppressant genes which results in failure of cell cycle checkpoints. Failure to stop damaged cells dividing, reducing apoptosis 

Finally, defects in DNA repair pathways 

## Evolutionary terms 
All development of cancer develops progressively through accumulation of mutations, selecting for advantageous variants. This is a form of evolution to a cell, however this doesnt benefit the organism and will typically lead to death.
