Comparison of [[Base excision repair]] and [[Nucleotide excision repair]]
For [[Base excision repair]]:
The first step is removal of the base, then backbone is cut on either side of the base 

For [[Nucleotide excision repair]]:
The first step is cutting the [[DNA]] backbone some way away from the lesion (5-8 bases).

![[Implications of Deamination of methyl cytosine.]]

Different errors are recognised by different glycosylases.
One glycosylases is specific for uracil, which results from deamination of cytosine. Simple, as Uracil does not belong in [[DNA]].

Another glycosylase always removes T in a GT pair because this mispairing results from [[Deamination]] of methyl cytosine. It must detect specifically T paired with G.


![[accuracy of Base excision repair]]


if DNA polymerase beta makes a mistake, the double helix will be slightly distorted 

Ligase is inefficient when there is a distortion 

Other systems can detect and repair the mistake

Unrepaired apurinated sites can be repaired by ![[translesion DNA polymerases]]. These often cause [[mutation]] because of the inaccuracy.

Uracil vs thymine 
Cytosine can spontaneously undergo [[Deamination]] to uracil. If uracil were a normal DNA base, repair systems could not distinguish real uracil from errors. 

5-methylcytosine (methylated form of cytosine) produces thymine, which can often be missed by [[Base excision repair]].
