We can show that the limit doesn't exist by demonstrating the existence of two limits. Because limits are unique, this means that it cant exist.

We establish first that for a continuous extension of $f$ to exist, it must have values over $\mathbb{R}^2$ including $(0,0)$ which is undefined currently. Thus our continuous extension must be the limit of $f$ at $(0,0)$. 

However, $\lim_{ (x,0) \to (0,0) }f(x,y)=\frac{x^2\cdot 0}{x^2 \cdot 0+ (x-0)^2}= 0$
and $\lim_{ (x,x) \to (0,0) }f(x,y)=\frac{x^4}{x^4+(x-x)^2}$ which is indeterminant form, so via L'hopitals 
$\lim_{ (x,x) \to (0,0) } \frac{3x^4}{3x^4}=1$
Thus, we have 2 limits that do not agree, and because limits must be unique, there is no limit at $(0,0)$ of $f$. Hence, there is no continuous extension of $f$.

b:
Write $\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u} \rangle = A + iB$ with
$$
A = \tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|^2\right), \qquad B = \tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{v}+i\mathbf{u}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{v}-i\mathbf{u}\|^2\right).
$$
Both are real, so $\overline{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u} \rangle} = A - iB$. We show the real part of $\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$ is $A$ and the imaginary part is $-B$.

**Real part.** By the parallelogram law, $\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^2 = 2\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) - \|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2$. Then
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right)
&= \tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \left(2\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) - \|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2\right)\right) \\
&= \tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\|^2 - \left(2\left(\|\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{u}\|^2\right) - \|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\|^2\right)\right) \\
&= \tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|^2\right) = A.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Imaginary part.** Two rewrites, using $|i| = 1$ and $\|-\mathbf{x}\| = \|\mathbf{x}\|$:
$$
\mathbf{u} + i\mathbf{v} = i(-i\mathbf{u} + \mathbf{v}), \qquad \mathbf{u} - i\mathbf{v} = i(-i\mathbf{u} - \mathbf{v}).
$$
Hence
$$
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right)
&= \tfrac{i}{4}\left(|i|^2\,\|\mathbf{v} - i\mathbf{u}\|^2 - |i|^2\,\|-\mathbf{v} - i\mathbf{u}\|^2\right) \\
&= \tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|\mathbf{v} - i\mathbf{u}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{v} + i\mathbf{u}\|^2\right) \\
&= -\tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|\mathbf{v} + i\mathbf{u}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{v} - i\mathbf{u}\|^2\right) = -iB.
\end{aligned}
$$

**Conclusion.**
$$
\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle = A - iB = \overline{A + iB} = \overline{\langle \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{u} \rangle}.
$$

We aim to show that $\langle  \mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v}\rangle+\langle \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w},\mathbf{v} \rangle=\langle 2\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v} \rangle$

Expanding both terms 
$$

\langle \mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{v} \rangle + \langle \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{v} \rangle\\= \tfrac14\left(\|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right)
+ \tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) \\
 + \tfrac14\left(\|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right)
+ \tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right).
$$

Real part:
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\tfrac14\left(\|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})-\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) \\
&= \tfrac14\left(\|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})+\mathbf{w}\|^2 + \|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{w}\|^2 - \left(\|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})+\mathbf{w}\|^2 + \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{w}\|^2\right)\right) && (\text{regrouping terms}) \\
&= \tfrac14\left(2\left(\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{w}\|^2\right) - 2\left(\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{w}\|^2\right)\right) && (\text{parallelogram law}) \\
&= \tfrac12\left(\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) \\
&= 2 \cdot \tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

Imaginary part:
$$
\tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w})-i\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w})-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right).
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
&= \tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|(\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v})+\mathbf{w}\|^2 + \|(\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{w}\|^2 - \left(\|(\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v})+\mathbf{w}\|^2 + \|(\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v})-\mathbf{w}\|^2\right)\right) && (\text{regrouping terms}) \\
&= \tfrac{i}{4}\left(2\left(\|\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{w}\|^2\right) - 2\left(\|\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{w}\|^2\right)\right) && (\text{parallelogram law}) \\
&= \tfrac{i}{2}\left(\|\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) \\
&= 2 \cdot \tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Conclusion.** Adding the real and imaginary parts:
$$
\langle \mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{v} \rangle + \langle \mathbf{u}-\mathbf{w}, \mathbf{v} \rangle
= 2\left[\tfrac14\left(\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|^2\right) + \tfrac{i}{4}\left(\|\mathbf{u}+i\mathbf{v}\|^2 - \|\mathbf{u}-i\mathbf{v}\|^2\right)\right]
= 2\langle \mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \rangle$$

**(b)** Define $\Phi(v) := \langle -, \iota(v) \rangle$. We show $\Phi(v)$ is linear, i.e. that $\Phi(v)(\lambda u + w) = \lambda\,\Phi(v)(u) + \Phi(v)(w)$. We check homogeneity and additivity separately.

**Homogeneity.** For a scalar $\lambda$ and vector $u$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(v)(\lambda u) &= \langle \lambda u, \iota(v) \rangle \\
&= \lambda \langle u, \iota(v) \rangle \text{ via homogeneity of inner products}\\
&= \lambda\, \Phi(v)(u).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Additivity.** For vectors $u, w$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi(v)(u + w) &= \langle u + w, \iota(v) \rangle \\
&= \langle u, \iota(v) \rangle + \langle w, \iota(v) \rangle\text{\ via additivity of inner products} \\
&= \Phi(v)(u) + \Phi(v)(w).
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $\Phi(v)$ is linear
