$\impliedby$
Let $T \in \mathcal{L}(V)$, and $V$ be a finite dimensional inner product space. 
Suppose there was some $U \subseteq V$ such that $T=P_{U}$ 
$\implies \forall \mathbf{v}\in V,\quad \exists \mathbf{u}\in U\ \ \exists \mathbf{w}\in U^\perp: \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u+w}$ 

So for $\mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}}\in V$
$\langle \mathbf{v_{1}},\mathbf{v_{2}} \rangle=\langle \mathbf{u_{1}}+\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2}+\mathbf{w}_{2} \rangle$
So, $\langle T\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2} \rangle=\langle \mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u_{2}}+\mathbf{w}_{2} \rangle$
$=\langle \mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2} \rangle+\langle \mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2} \rangle$
$= \langle \mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2} \rangle$  (via definition of orthogonal complement)
And because $T=P_{U}$, $T(\mathbf{v}_{2})=\mathbf{u}_{2}$
So $\langle T\mathbf{v}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2} \rangle=\langle \mathbf{v}_{1},T\mathbf{v}_{2} \rangle$ so $T$ is self adjoint.


$\implies$
Let $T \in \mathcal{L}(V)$, be self adjoint. 
Notice that $V=\ker(T) \oplus range(T)$
$\implies V=\ker(T^*) \oplus range(T)$     ($T$ is self adjoint)
Note as well that $\ker(T^*) =range(T)^\perp$
Let $\mathbf{v}\in V$
$\implies \exists \mathbf{u} \in \ker(T^*)^\perp,\ \ \exists \mathbf{w}\in range(T)$ such that $\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{w}$
$\implies T(\mathbf{v})=T(\mathbf{u})+T(\mathbf{w})$
$\implies T(\mathbf{v})=T(\mathbf{w})$
Because $\mathbf{w}\in Range(T),\exists \mathbf{x}\in V$ such that $T(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}$
$\implies T(\mathbf{v})=T(T(\mathbf{x}))$
But because $T^2=T$
$T\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}$ 
Thus, $T=P_{U}$, where $U=range(T)$
