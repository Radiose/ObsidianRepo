iii) 
No. They are not entitled to say this. This is because we have trained only on a sample of all possible encounters. The observed error being zero only tells us about the likelihood the actual error is small.

What we are able to say is how low the error is likely to be. To do this, we would need the size of the sample $N$, we would need the empirical risk, and a confidence level. Mathematically, this is captured by generalization bounds such as $E_\text{out}(h) \leq E_\text{in}(h) + \epsilon(N, v, \delta)$, where $\epsilon$ shrinks as $N$ grows but is never exactly zero for finite $N$.

With a sample size of 20,000 and an empirical error rate of 0, they would be able to claim that the true error rate lies less than some given $\epsilon$ within the empirical error, and with some confidence $1-\delta$.

In other words, they can provide some upper bound on swoop prediction error, not a certainty.
