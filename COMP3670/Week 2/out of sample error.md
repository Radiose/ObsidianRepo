> [!definition] Out-of-sample error
> The **out-of-sample error** of a hypothesis $h$ is the expected loss under $P$,
> $$
> L(h) = \mathbb{E}\big[\ell((\mathbf{X}, Y), h)\big] = \int \ell((\mathbf{x}, y), h)\, dP(\mathbf{x}, y).
> $$

- With the **squared loss**, $L(h) = \mathbb{E}[(h(\mathbf{X}) - Y)^2]$: the expected squared distance between $y$ and $h(\mathbf{x})$ for a point $(\mathbf{x}, y)$ generated by $P$
- With the **binary loss**, $L(h) = \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{1}\{h(\mathbf{X}) \neq Y\}] = \mathbb{P}(h(\mathbf{X}) \neq Y)$: the probability of misclassifying a point $(\mathbf{x}, y)$ generated by $P$ when using $h$


We should note here that this formula is rarely able to be achieved, because $P$ is typically never fully known.
