## Reliability
Inductive reasoning is not completely reliable. While we can explain a pattern inductively, we often require real [[hypothetico-deductive reasoning]] to actually explain why it holds. 
Much of science alternates between the two but generally, 
[[hypothetico-deductive reasoning]] is safe. The nature of an [[The conditional statement|implication]] means that if the premises are true, then the conclusion must be true. However, no finite number of inductive observations can logically guarantee a universal claim. 

This is known as Humes problem of induction

A good example of this is *the black swan*
 - [[inductive reasoning]] assumed that all swans were white 
 - one counterexample of a black swam completely disproved the theory.
